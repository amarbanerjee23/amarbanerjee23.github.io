Academic innovation programs

Do not just prepare students to finish the syllabus. Prepare them to find what the syllabus does not answer.

The programs are designed around three future-facing capabilities: innovative thinking, research problem discovery and invention thinking. Students practise these capabilities on real problems and leave with outputs they can continue after the session.

01 · Core student program

Innovation Thinking Lab

For students who are used to receiving problem statements and need to learn how to discover, frame and challenge problems themselves.

Best forUndergraduate and postgraduate students · interdisciplinary cohorts · innovation cells · hackathon preparation · capstone/project groups

The shift

BEFORE

“Tell me the problem. I will build a solution.”

AFTER

“I can recognise a meaningful problem, explain why it matters and test whether my idea is worth pursuing.”

What students practise

Problem sensing

Observe friction, unmet needs, contradictions and assumptions.

Problem framing

Turn a broad topic into a sharp, valuable question.

Structured creativity

Use reverse assumptions, contradiction thinking, analogies and recombination.

Idea stress-testing

Challenge novelty, feasibility, value, ethics and failure modes.

Evidence design

Define the smallest experiment that can strengthen or kill an idea.

Innovation communication

Defend the problem, insight and evidence to a skeptical audience.

Student-owned outputsProblem opportunity map · reframed challenge · idea portfolio · experiment canvas · innovation pitch
02 · Research excellence track

From Burning Problem to Top-Venue Research

For students and early researchers who should learn to choose the research problem before worrying about the paper template.

Best forMasters and PhD scholars · research clubs · final-year projects · faculty-guided research cohorts

The central question

Why would a selective journal or conference care about this problem, this contribution and this evidence?

A literature gap is useful, but a gap by itself is not a guarantee of importance. Students learn to look for consequential, timely and technically defensible problems, then connect the contribution to the expectations of the venues they aspire to.

What the track covers

Burning problem discovery

Identify unresolved questions with scientific, societal or industry consequence.

Venue-aware reading

Read top journals and conferences to understand what strong contributions look like.

Novelty map

Separate “not done before” from a contribution that is meaningful and non-obvious.

Reviewer-first design

Anticipate obvious rejection questions before the experiments are complete.

Evidence architecture

Choose baselines, experiments, ablations, validation and limitations that match the claim.

Paper story

Write a clear contribution narrative rather than assembling disconnected sections.

The ambitionMove from “publish somewhere” toward work that is better prepared for selective, high-quality journals and conferences. No venue acceptance is guaranteed; the objective is to raise the quality, rigor and fit of the work.
03 · Invention and IP track

Patent Thinking Lab

For students who have built something interesting but do not yet know how to recognise invention, prior art or a defensible inventive contribution.

Best forCapstone teams · innovation clubs · research scholars · incubation cells · student startup teams

What students learn

Prior-art mindset

Search the technical idea space, not just the exact keywords used in the project.

Novelty

Understand whether the proposed invention has already been disclosed.

Non-obviousness

Ask whether the difference is merely new or genuinely inventive.

Claims thinking

Describe the inventive concept as a system of essential elements and relationships.

Paper vs patent

Recognise when publication should wait until IP options are considered.

Research-to-IP roadmap

Translate project observations into invention candidates and next validation steps.

OutputsPrior-art search plan · invention canvas · novelty/non-obviousness questions · paper-vs-patent decision map
